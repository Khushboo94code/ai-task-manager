# Due Date and Overdue Policy

## Setting Due Dates
All high priority tasks must have a due date. Medium and low priority tasks should have a due date when possible. A task without a due date is assumed to have no hard deadline, but it is still expected to be completed within a reasonable time.

## What Counts as Overdue
A task is overdue if its due date has passed and its status is not completed. Tasks are considered overdue at midnight on the due date. There is no grace period.

## Overdue Escalation Rules
Overdue tasks automatically escalate in urgency:
- An overdue low priority task should be treated as medium priority.
- An overdue medium priority task should be treated as high priority.
- An overdue high priority task is a critical incident and must be escalated to the team lead immediately.

## What To Do With Overdue Tasks
1. First reassess if the task is still needed. If no longer relevant, mark it completed with a note.
2. If still needed, update the due date to a realistic new date.
3. Escalate the priority according to the rules above.
4. Notify the responsible team member in standup.

## Changing Due Dates
Due dates can be changed by any team member but a reason should be added to the description. Repeatedly changing due dates on the same task is a signal that the task is too large and should be broken down.
